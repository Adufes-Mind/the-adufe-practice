Who this is for

This practice was built
for three people.

🌿

The trauma survivor ready to rebuild

You have survived something significant. You function — sometimes brilliantly. And you are exhausted by the distance between the life you're living and the one you know is possible. You are ready to close that distance.

🔥

The high-achiever falling apart beneath the surface

From the outside, everything looks fine. Inside, you are carrying a weight that no amount of productivity, success, or performance can touch. You know something needs to change. You just don't yet know how.

🏛

The organisation that takes its people seriously

You understand that your team's wellbeing is not separate from your organisation's performance. Drawing on a systems thinking approach, I work with you to build the structures and culture where people can be present, honest, and genuinely well — not just functional.

The framework

Five stages. One direction.
Toward the whole self.

Stage I

Witness

Before anything can change, it must be seen — clearly, without flinching, with full compassion.

Stage II

Name

Language is medicine. Giving precise words to what you carry shifts everything.

Stage III

Feel

You cannot think your way through feelings that were felt into you. We go through, not around.

Stage IV

Choose

In the space that healing creates, you begin to consciously design the self you are becoming.

Stage V

Build

Healing is not the destination. It is the foundation. This is where you begin to create.

About this practice

I built this
because I needed
it to exist.

Esther "Adufe" Omoyele

I am a trauma-informed support practitioner, mental wellness educator, and storyteller based in Abuja, Nigeria. I hold a Diploma in Psychodynamic Psychotherapy and have completed WHO training in Mental Health, Human Rights and Recovery. My practice is grounded in a systems thinking approach, alongside over a year of supervised clinical experience through direct support work.

I came to this work through the long, nonlinear, deeply personal route of having lived what I now teach. I am a trauma survivor. I know what it costs to keep functioning while something important goes unaddressed. I know the particular exhaustion of performing wellness rather than practicing it. I know what it feels like to understand your patterns intellectually while remaining, on a felt level, unable to change them.

I built this practice because I needed something like it to exist — and because I believe that the people of this world, regardless of where they are, deserve access to this quality of support.

My work draws from trauma-informed therapy, somatic practice, attachment theory, identity psychology, and a deeply African understanding of wellbeing — the knowledge that healing is not individual. It is relational, communal, and purposeful.

I am also a screenwriter, storyteller, and researcher. My screenwriting has been acknowledged both at home and internationally. I have contributed to international mental health research as a field team lead in Nigeria. And I write weekly in The Adufe Letter — a newsletter read by people in over twenty countries.

All of these things are part of the same work: helping people understand themselves, articulate their experience, and build lives that are genuinely theirs.

What I believe

The philosophy
behind the practice.

🌱

Healing is not the destination

It is the foundation. People do not heal so that they can stop — they heal so that they can finally begin. Every piece of work I do is oriented toward what becomes possible on the other side of it.

🤝

Healing is relational

You cannot fully heal alone. The quality of the relationship between practitioner and client is not incidental to the work — it is the work. I bring my whole self to this, not a clinical persona.

🌍

Lived experience is expertise

I do not approach trauma from the outside. I bring my own years of navigating it — personally and professionally — as a form of knowledge that academic training alone cannot produce.

🗣

Language is medicine

You cannot heal what you cannot name. A significant part of the work I do is giving precise, honest language to experiences that have been lived without words — and watching what shifts when people finally have them.

🔒

Transparency is non-negotiable

I disclose my training and scope of practice clearly. I work under supervision and maintain clear referral pathways. I will always tell you exactly what I can and cannot offer — because the trust between us is the foundation of everything else.

✦

You already have what you need

I do not fix people. I create conditions — through presence, through honest support, through a structured framework — in which people can access the wholeness that was always already there.

Referral pathways

When you need more
than this practice can offer.

Trauma-informed support has real limits — it is not a substitute for clinical or psychiatric care. If, during our work together, it becomes clear that you need diagnosis, medication, or a higher level of clinical care, I will say so directly and help you find the right door. Below are organisations I trust for referrals in Nigeria, alongside guidance for clients based elsewhere.

Crisis & counselling support

Mentally Aware Nigeria Initiative (MANI) — 24/7 free, confidential crisis support and referral to therapy for young people across Nigeria.

Crisis & counselling support

She Writes Woman — 24/7 toll-free helpline (0800 800 2000) and teletherapy for anyone in Nigeria.

Clinical / psychiatric care

Federal Neuropsychiatric Hospital, Yaba, Lagos — federal psychiatric hospital for in-person diagnosis and treatment.

Clinical / psychiatric care

Lagos University Teaching Hospital (LUTH), Department of Psychiatry, Idi-Araba — teaching hospital psychiatric care.

Telepsychiatry & medication management

Happy Family Hospital — telepsychiatry serving all 36 states plus Nigerians abroad, with in-person clinics in Lagos and Abuja.

International clients

Please seek a licensed therapist or psychiatrist registered in your own country — for example, through a national or regional professional directory, your health insurer, or your primary care doctor. I am glad to help you think through what to look for, even if I cannot make the referral myself.

If you are in immediate danger or crisis, please contact your local emergency services or the nearest hospital emergency department right away.

Cancellation & rescheduling policy

More than 48 hours' notice

Full flexibility. Reschedule to another available time at no charge, or cancel for a full refund to your original payment method.

24–48 hours' notice

One complimentary reschedule is available. If cancelling outright, 50% of the session fee is credited toward a future session; the remaining 50% covers the reserved time.

Under 24 hours' notice, or no-show

The full session fee is forfeited, as the time was held exclusively for you. Genuine emergencies are considered case by case — please reach out as soon as you can.

If Adufe needs to reschedule

You will be offered first right to the next available slot at no extra cost, or a full refund if no suitable time can be found.

Multi-session packages & corporate bookings

Each session within the Intensive or Retainer follows the same notice windows above. For corporate sessions (Lunch & Learn, Executive Support, Team Series), please allow 7 days' notice for a full refund and 72 hours' for a 50% refund, given the logistics involved in preparing these sessions.
